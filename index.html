About

I am starting a Ph.D. in Computer Science at the University of Illinois Chicago in Fall 2026, advised by Prof. V.N. Venkatakrishnan, where I will work on provenance-based detection of advanced persistent threats.

I am finishing an M.S. in Data Science (thesis track) at UMass Dartmouth, where I have been a Graduate Research Assistant with Dr. Gokhan Kul on a Department of Defense funded project (Grant W911NF-22-2-0160) in collaboration with the U.S. Military Academy at West Point. My thesis, "From Threat Intelligence to Decision Theory: Empirically Grounded Utility Functions for Adversarial Risk Analysis in Network Intrusion Detection," brings together two contributions: MITRE ATT&CK attack chain prediction using hybrid LSTM-Markov models, and ARA-OSID, a decision-theoretic framework with ATT&CK-derived utility functions for defender-attacker scenarios in open-set intrusion detection.

A common thread runs through this work. Detectors are usually evaluated as if the world were static and the attacker indifferent. Neither holds. My earlier work named the False Champion Problem: intrusion detection models that post excellent aggregate accuracy while failing on exactly the attack classes that matter. The decision-theoretic work that followed asks what a defender should actually do once that uncertainty is priced in. Provenance graphs are the natural next substrate for the same question, which is what draws me to the work at UIC.

Before graduate school I spent three years as a data scientist and software engineer building production ML pipelines and cloud services.
